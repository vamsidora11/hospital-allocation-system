# Emergency Protocols

Policy ID: EMRG-PROT-01
Effective date: 2026-01-15
Owner: Emergency Department Medical Director

## 1. Priority classification

Every incoming emergency case is assigned a priority level on arrival, independent of arrival
order.

### 1.1 Priority-1 criteria

Cardiac, respiratory, or trauma presentations with unstable vitals are classified Priority 1 and
accepted regardless of current department load. Priority 1 acceptance is not subject to the
diversion thresholds in Section 2.3.

### 1.2 Priority-2 criteria

High-acuity presentations without immediately unstable vitals — for example, respiratory distress
with stable oxygen saturation, or a moderate trauma mechanism without confirmed unstable
injury — are classified Priority 2. Priority 2 cases are accepted under normal load and evaluated
against diversion thresholds only when the department is at or above limited-capacity status.

### 1.3 Priority-3 and Priority-4 criteria

Moderate and minor presentations without acute instability are classified Priority 3 or 4
respectively. These are the categories eligible for diversion under Section 2.3.

## 2. Load-based routing

### 2.1 Load monitoring

Department load is tracked continuously as a percentage of staffed treatment-bay capacity and
categorized as normal (below 80%), constrained (80–91%), or limited (92% and above).

### 2.2 Acceptance defaults

Under normal and constrained load, all priority levels are accepted on-site. Priority 1 and
Priority 2 cases remain accepted on-site even under limited load.

### 2.3 Diversion thresholds

Minor-acuity cases (Priority 4) may be diverted to urgent care when emergency department load
exceeds the limited-capacity threshold defined in Section 2.1. Priority 3 cases may be diverted
only if load remains at limited capacity after Priority 4 diversion and no specialist bottleneck
is resolved by the diversion.

## 3.0 Regional transfer

Cases requiring specialist capability unavailable on-site are transferred to the designated
regional facility. This applies independently of department load: a case may be transferred even
under normal load if the required specialist capability — for example, a specific surgical
subspecialty — is not available at this facility. Transfer decisions require confirmation that the
receiving facility has accepted the patient before transport begins.

## 4. Mass-casualty coordination

Mass-casualty incidents are governed jointly with Section 1.0 of the Compliance Rules, which
defines activation and parallel-processing requirements. This section addresses only the
priority-assignment rules above as they apply once mass-casualty activation is declared: each
patient in a mass-casualty incident still receives an individual priority classification under
Section 1, ordered by acuity rather than arrival order.

## 5. Documentation

Every routing decision under this policy — accept, transfer, or divert — is logged with the
priority level, the load status at time of decision, and, for transfers, the receiving facility's
confirmation.
