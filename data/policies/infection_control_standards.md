# Infection Control Standards

Policy ID: INFC-STD-01
Effective date: 2026-01-15
Owner: Infection Prevention and Control

## 1. Isolation categories

### 1.1 General precautions

Standard precautions apply to all patients regardless of isolation status and are not sufficient
on their own for a patient meeting any criterion in Section 1.2.

### 1.2 Isolation categories

Isolation status is assigned under one of three categories: contact, droplet, or airborne/
respiratory, based on the suspected or confirmed transmission route of the presenting condition.

### 1.3 Respiratory isolation

Respiratory distress with suspected infectious origin requires a negative-pressure isolation room
where available. If no negative-pressure room is available, place the patient in a standard
isolation room per the Bed Allocation Policy (Section 4.1) and apply airborne precautions pending
room reassignment, and log the deviation for review.

## 2. Staffing for isolation care

### 2.1 PPE requirements

Staff entering an isolation room must don the personal protective equipment appropriate to the
assigned isolation category before entry, and doff it before exiting, following the facility's
standard donning/doffing sequence.

### 2.2 Isolation staffing

Isolation-room patients require a nurse credentialed in respiratory or infectious care for the
duration of stay. This credential requirement is independent of, and in addition to, the
nurse-to-patient ratio defined in Section 4.2 of the Staff Scheduling Policy. A shift that cannot
meet this credential requirement must escalate per Section 6.0 of the Staff Scheduling Policy
rather than staff the room with a non-credentialed nurse.

## 3. Environmental controls

Isolation rooms are inspected each shift to confirm air-handling status matches the assigned
isolation category. A room with a failed air-handling check is taken out of isolation rotation
until remediated, regardless of current bed pressure.

## 4. De-escalation

### 4.1 Criteria for de-escalation

A patient may be moved out of isolation once confirmatory testing rules out the suspected
transmissible condition, or once the clinically defined isolation period has elapsed, whichever
the attending documents first.

### 4.2 Documentation

De-escalation from isolation is logged with the confirming test result or elapsed-period
justification, and the freed isolation room is returned to available status only after
environmental turnover is complete.
