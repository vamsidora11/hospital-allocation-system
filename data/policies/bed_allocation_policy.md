# Bed Allocation Policy

Policy ID: BED-ALLOC-01
Effective date: 2026-01-15
Owner: Patient Placement Committee

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy governs how incoming and transferring patients are matched to a bed type. It applies
to the emergency department, direct admissions, and internal transfers between units. It does not
cover outpatient scheduling or elective admission bed holds, which are governed separately.

## 2. Bed categories

The facility recognizes three bed categories for allocation purposes: General Ward, Isolation
Room, and Intensive Care Unit (ICU).

### 2.1 General Ward

General Ward beds support patients who do not require continuous monitoring or isolation. Nursing
checks occur at a standard interval appropriate to the unit.

### 2.2 Isolation Room

Isolation Rooms are single-occupancy rooms with independent air handling, reserved for patients
with suspected or confirmed transmissible conditions.

### 2.3 ICU

ICU beds provide continuous physiological monitoring and are staffed to a higher nurse-to-patient
ratio than General Ward, as defined in the Staff Scheduling Policy.

### 2.4 General ward defaults

Patients who do not meet the critical-acuity criteria in Section 3.2 and do not meet the isolation
criteria in Section 4.1 default to General Ward placement. Placement decisions should not
default to a higher-acuity bed type without a documented clinical justification, since doing so
reduces capacity available to patients who need it.

## 3. Critical and monitored placement

### 3.1 Monitoring-level indicators

Indicators supporting elevated monitoring include unstable vital signs, a documented critical
diagnosis category, requirement for continuous cardiac or respiratory monitoring, or recent
resuscitation.

### 3.2 Critical acuity placement

Patients presenting with critical acuity indicators are placed in monitored ICU beds ahead of
ward assignment. This includes, without limitation, acute myocardial infarction, unresolved
respiratory failure, sepsis with hemodynamic instability, and post-resuscitation care. Critical
acuity placement takes precedence over ward capacity constraints; if no ICU bed is immediately
available, escalate per Section 5.2 rather than placing the patient in General Ward.

## 4. Isolation placement

### 4.1 Isolation criteria

Suspected or confirmed infectious status requires isolation-room placement regardless of ward
capacity. Isolation status is determined by the presenting diagnosis category, known exposure
history, or a positive screening result. Isolation placement is not overridden by bed-availability
pressure; if no isolation room is open, escalate per Section 5.2.

### 4.2 Isolation with elevated monitoring

A patient who meets both the critical-acuity criteria in Section 3.2 and the isolation criteria
in Section 4.1 is placed in an ICU bed with isolation precautions applied, coordinated with the
Staff Scheduling Policy's isolation-staffing requirement (Section 2.2 of the Infection Control
Standards).

## 5. Capacity management and escalation

### 5.1 Occupancy thresholds

Bed occupancy is considered constrained at 80% and limited at 92% or above, by category. These
thresholds inform, but do not override, the placement rules in Sections 3 and 4.

### 5.2 Escalation when the indicated bed type is unavailable

When the clinically indicated bed type has no immediate capacity, escalate to the on-call bed
manager and, for ICU or isolation shortfalls, consider regional transfer per the Emergency
Protocols (Section 3.0). Do not substitute a lower-acuity bed type to resolve a capacity shortfall
without documented sign-off.

## 6. Review and exceptions

Any placement made outside this policy's default rules must be documented with a clinical
rationale and is subject to review by the Patient Placement Committee at its next scheduled
session. This policy is reviewed annually or upon material change to facility capacity.
